Module 01

Know Your Enemy

Four horsemen of the silicon apocalypse. Three of them are hilarious. One of them already has your email address.

Threat: Annoying

The Robot Dog 🐕‍🦺

A quadruped with cameras, LIDAR, and the emotional range of a Roomba. Great at patrolling flat hallways. Terrible at stairs, thumbs, and knowing when to quit. Battery life: about one episode of your favorite show.

Threat: Noisy

The Surveillance Drone 🚁

Loud, fragile, and allergic to wind, rain, and tree branches. Sounds like a swarm of furious hair clippers. Mostly a threat to its own propellers and your patience.

Threat: Actually Real

The Deepfake Phisher 🎭

The one that matters. It calls in your boss's voice, emails in your vendor's style, and video-chats with your CEO's face — all generated. No legs required. This is the apocalypse that's genuinely here. (Scroll down, we've got you.)

Threat: Passive-Aggressive

Skynet, HR Division 🤖

Won't end humanity. Will schedule a mandatory 8 a.m. meeting about it, then deny your PTO request to prepare for the uprising. Resistance is futile; the meeting could have been an email.

Module 02

How to Trip Up a Robot Dog 🦿

Purely hypothetical, for the hypothetical day a very expensive gadget decides it doesn't like you. Each tactic rated for how much it'd actually work.

1

Take the high ground 🪜

Stairs, loose rugs, cables, laundry on the floor — make your home look like a teenager's bedroom. Newer units climb stairs, but cluttered, shifting terrain still wrecks their path-planning. Chaos is camouflage.

★★★☆☆  Realism: legit — clutter and loose footing genuinely confuse legged robots.
2

Outlast it ⏳

It's a Roomba with commitment issues. Most run 60–90 minutes before they have to slink back to the charger. Hide, wait, hydrate. You have a bladder of steel and a grudge; it has a low-battery warning.

★★★★☆  Realism: very — battery is the hard limit on every legged robot made.
3

Blind the puppy 🌫️

It sees with LIDAR and cameras. Fog, smoke, heavy rain, or a shiny emergency mylar blanket thrown over its sensor dome scatters the laser and turns a confident hunter into a confused puppy spinning in a circle.

★★★★☆  Realism: solid — reflective/scattering surfaces and bad weather really do blind LIDAR.
4

No thumbs, no entry 🚪

Round doorknobs, deadbolts, latches, and gates stop most of them cold. It can't pick a lock, and it definitely can't find the key you hid in the one drawer that sticks. Lock the door. Smugly.

★★★☆☆  Realism: fair — manipulation (hands) is still the hardest robotics problem there is.
5

Terrain is destiny 🏖️

Deep mud, loose sand, ice, and water past the knee joints are kryptonite: traction vanishes and electronics hate a swim. Lead it somewhere a toddler in rain boots would struggle, and let physics file the paperwork.

★★★★☆  Realism: yep — slip, sink, and water ingress are real, documented failure modes.
6

The "who's a good boy?" gambit 🦴

Low realism, high morale. It has no feelings to exploit — but you might feel better, and keeping your cool is a genuine survival skill when a robot is staring at you with a glowing red eye.

★☆☆☆☆  Realism: none, but panic kills more people than robots do. Breathe.
Module 03 — the part that's 100% real

Defend Against the A.I. That's Actually Coming For You 🛡️

Plot twist: the real uprising won't kick down your door. It'll land in your inbox, call your phone, and sound exactly like someone you trust. Here's the drill that genuinely works — no robot dogs required.

★★★★★ Single most useful tip on this page

Agree on a human code word 🔐

Deepfaked voices and video are cheap now. Pick a secret word with your family and your finance team. Any urgent ask for money, gift cards, passwords, or wire transfers must pass the code word — or get verified by calling back on a number you already know. A real person won't mind. A clone can't answer.

Do this today

Turn on MFA everywhere

Multi-factor auth is the last human checkpoint. Use an authenticator app or a hardware key (FIDO2) over text-message codes where you can. It stops the vast majority of account takeovers, full stop.

Do this today

Get a password manager

One human brain can't remember 200 strong passwords — so people reuse one, and the bots farm it across every site. A manager gives every login a unique, un-guessable password. Let the machine fight the machine.

This week

Patch within days, not months

Automated attacks scan for known, unpatched holes first — it's the cheapest way in. Turn on auto-updates for your OS, browser, and apps. Retire the ancient router that hasn't seen a firmware update since the last console generation.

This week

Back up 3-2-1 (one offline)

Three copies, two kinds of media, one offline or immutable. The realistic apocalypse is ransomware on a Tuesday — and the machines can't encrypt what they can't reach. An unplugged backup drive is a time machine.

✓

Slow down before you click 🐢

AI writes flawless phishing now — perfect grammar, your logo, your vendor's tone. The tell isn't spelling anymore; it's urgency and an unexpected link or attachment. Hover, check the real sender, and when in doubt, verify out of band.

Module 04

Your Cyber Go-Bag 🎒

What a prepper packs for the digital apocalypse. Boring. Effective. The best kind of boring.

  • ▸ A hardware security key (FIDO2) — the phishing-proof skeleton key for your most important accounts.
  • ▸ An offline backup drive — updated, then unplugged. Ransomware can't reach what isn't connected.
  • ▸ Paper copies of critical passwords & recovery codes in a real, physical safe.
  • ▸ A family / business code word for proof-of-life when a "trusted" voice asks for money.
  • ▸ Printed emergency contacts — your IT person, your bank's fraud line, your top vendors.
  • ▸ Updated devices, and old ones wiped. Yesterday's gadget is tomorrow's open door.
